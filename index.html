How a trip comes together

Drivers post trips they were taking anyway. Riders claim a seat. Nobody makes a detour they did not agree to.

  1. A driver posts the trip

    Origin, destination, date and distance. DRIVAH works out the per-seat fuel share automatically, so nobody has to haggle over Venmo.

  2. Riders claim the empty seats

    Search by where you're leaving from or heading to. You see the driver's name, rating, car and how many rides they've done before you commit.

  3. The seat is held the moment you tap

    Seat counts update instantly and the driver is notified. Two people tapping the last seat at once cannot both get it.

  4. You travel, and split the fuel

    Meet at the pickup point, split the cost, done. Plans change — you can leave a ride and your seat goes straight back to the pool.

🐊 UF VERIFIED

@ufl.edu or you're not in

Registration requires a working University of Florida address. No public sign-ups, no accounts from outside the university.

Your UFID never sits in a database

It is hashed on our server the moment you register, with a secret that never reaches your browser. The raw number is discarded before storage — we could not tell you your own UFID if you asked.

Drivers register their car

Make, model, year and plate, shown to riders before they claim a seat. Vehicles must be 2010 or newer, and drivers confirm they carry their own insurance.

Cancellations reach people

If a driver calls off a trip, everyone booked on it is told immediately rather than finding out at the kerb.

Questions worth asking

Is this just Uber with extra steps?

No — the money works differently. A rideshare driver is working, and the fare covers their time plus the platform's cut. A DRIVAH driver is going somewhere anyway, and riders chip in for fuel. The cost is calculated at the IRS mileage rate and split between everyone in the car, so the driver ends up out of pocket less, not in profit.

What does a trip actually cost?

Distance × $0.21, divided by the number of riders, with a $1.50 floor so very short hops are still worth posting. Gainesville to Orlando split three ways works out around $8 a seat. The slider at the top of this page runs the same formula the app does.

Do I need a car to sign up?

No. Most people join as riders and never post a trip. If you do drive, you can register as a driver and post the trips you were already making.

How do I pay?

Directly with your driver, however you two normally settle up. DRIVAH calculates the fair share and tracks what you've spent and saved; it does not sit in the middle of the payment.

What if my plans change?

Leave the ride from the app and your seat immediately returns to the pool for someone else. Please do it early rather than at the last minute — the driver is a classmate, not a company.

Is it only Gainesville?

For now, yes — DRIVAH is UF students only while we get the basics right. The verification is built to open up to other universities later.
